# ACT IV: THE WAR ROOM

*Feeding the Algorithm, an ad so native nobody knew it was an ad, a founder video on take forty-one, a landing page for the landing page, a descent into the settings to find a lever that doesn't exist, ten phone calls, and Thanksgiving.* Target: 12 to 16 minutes, 8 to 11 decisions. Day 33 to Day 1 (Thanksgiving).

**Route:** set the Q4 traditions in motion (creative, the founder video, the website, the holdout) → survive the Descent → *call customers* (optional, but it's the hidden ending) → build and lock the offer → **the Blackout, Thanksgiving afternoon** → midnight.

Load `world/the-lever.md` now. Start with the market weather (`rules.md` §5), and the Enhancement of the Act (`rules.md` §15): in one ad, "AI image expansion" has given the sponge a small pair of hands. It's the best-performing ad of the year. Nobody is allowed to talk about it.

## 4.1 THE WHITEBOARD

Wrung HQ, blinds down. **Margo** has written one line at the top: ***DOES IT SURVIVE CONTRIBUTION MARGIN?*** Under it, Kyle has written forty tactics, a drawing of a hexagon, and the word *"Portuguese?"* Dot is here, if she was recruited, with a stack of notebooks.

**The goal, said plainly by Margo:** *"By Thanksgiving: one offer, one email plan, one ad plan, and a number I believe. Everything on this board earns its place or it's gone."*

**The Q4 traditions** happen whether anyone wants them or not. Show them in all their glorious stupidity: an **hourly pacing spreadsheet** with 2,016 cells; an **ugly Christmas-tree static** (the most effective ad format in the industry, which every creative director privately hates); and the Feed posting *"we're debriefing last November"* while also posting *"we started this Q4 in August."* Time matters now: each piece costs days (`world/the-feed.md`).

## 4.2 FEEDING THE ALGORITHM

A notification arrives, then another, then nine more:

```
Advertisers like you see better results with 50+ creatives.
Your ad set is in Learning Limited.
Want us to make 400 variations? We've made 400 variations.
```

**The Algorithm hungers** (`rules.md` §15). Three ways to feed it:
- **The 400.** Kyle's AI tool makes 400 ads overnight (STACK +1, 2 days, then 3 days of cleanup when 380 of them are subtly wrong: sponges with six corners, a model holding the sponge to her ear like a phone, a lifestyle shot of a sponge at a wedding). The Algorithm is happy for one day.
- **The napkin.** **Tess Varga**, by video call, writes three openers on a napkin and holds it up to the camera. *"Did you read the brief."* (4 days, and they're good.) The Algorithm goes quiet, the way a cat goes quiet. CPMs drop a little, and nobody knows why yet (it's the Lever, 4.6).
- **Ads that don't look like ads.** Kyle makes the purest one ever made: a grainy vacation photo with no product, no logo, no words. It gets two million views. It sells zero sponges, because nobody knew it was an ad, including the Algorithm. Kyle wants to make forty more.

*Who gets the credit:* whatever works, Kyle posts about it as a media-buying win. Whatever doesn't, he calls "creative fatigue." Tess, if she sees it, says nothing, and adds Kyle to a list.

## 4.3 THE FOUNDER VIDEO

The Feed agrees: in Q4, the brand needs a **founder video**. Selfie, in the car, "raw", "unscripted", *"I don't usually do this, but."* Play the shoot: the player in the driver's seat, Kyle directing from the passenger side, take after take. *"Less polished. No, less. Can you be more tired?"* By take 41, they've accidentally said something true, and that's the one that works.
- A Creative's Hook nails it in one take. A Founder's genuine story about the kitchen where the first sponge was cut makes Dot cry. An Operator who just says the price and the free gift gets the best click-through of the season and is furious about it.

## 4.4 THE LANDING PAGE QUESTION

**Lenny Szabo** looks at the product page for nine seconds. *"Your ads are fine. It's the website."* The page takes 6 seconds to load on a phone, because fourteen apps (one of them Halcyon) each add a script. Kyle proposes a landing page. Then a landing page for the landing page, to "pre-sell" it. Then an advertorial before that. Margo draws the funnel on the whiteboard and it's longer than the whiteboard.
- **Fixing the actual product page** (3 days, cutting apps) heals MARGIN one level and lowers STACK. Building the landing-page tower raises STACK and does, admittedly, convert slightly better, for reasons nobody can isolate.

## 4.5 THE OHIO HOLDOUT

**Walt** or **Hank Dorsey** (on a call, or on the Feed) suggests it: turn off all ads in one state for two weeks and see what actually changes. *"We've done this quarter before. Test it."* It needs **14 days**. Started by about Day 16, the result lands before Black Friday.

The result (`DISCOVER_INCREMENTALITY`): the platform said it drove 60 percent of revenue. Ohio fell by **25 percent**. (Ohio was also the state buying the Portuguese ads. Nobody can explain this either.) The ads matter. Less than the dashboard says. Email matters more. Margo frames the result.
- **Kyle's moment** (`ALLY_KYLE_UNSUBSCRIBES`): after the holdout, or after the Descent, Kyle quietly cancels every course, asks Scale Academy to take his face off the sales page, unfollows the Professor, and turns off his automated rules. *"I'm going to run the account like it's my money."*

**Podcast offer 3** (`rules.md` §12): two microphones and a foam panel appear on Kyle's desk, "just to test it". He has a name ready. It's a sponge pun. He's so proud of it.

## 4.7 TEN PHONE CALLS

**Always offer it:** after the Descent or the holdout, make it option A of the next menu (*"Have Dot get ten customers on the phone, and actually call them"*), since players rarely think of it. It costs one day. No guru at ScaleFest has done this in years.

- With Dot's help (or alone; it's harder), the player calls ten real customers. Not a survey. A conversation. Ask why they buy, and wait.
- A sincere day of calls is `SOCIAL_CUSTOMER_CALLS`, and lowers STACK by one. The truth of it is `DISCOVER_WHY_THEY_BUY`: four of the ten say a version of the same thing. *"Oh, I don't use them. I send them to my son. His sponge could walk on its own."* Dot's notebooks confirm it: **41 percent of orders are parents sending sponges to grown-up kids who've moved out.** Nobody buys Wrung because of a hook, a hexagon or a dubstep symphony. They buy it out of love, mild disgust, and the fact that it doesn't smell. Black Friday is gifting season.
- **Dot's moment** (`ALLY_DOT_PRESENTS`): if Dot is recruited, she takes the marker. *"Can I say something? I've been waiting four years to say something."* She draws a sponge, an arrow, and a house. Everybody gets it at once. Kyle asks if it's a framework. Dot: *"It's a mom, Kyle."*

Now the plan. Run `game/puzzles.md`, *Puzzle 3: The Offer*: three offers are on the whiteboard, each from a different guru; the player picks one, merges them, or builds their own, and it has to survive contribution margin. **Locking it** sets `plan_locked` (note the day: `ACH_PLAN_BY_HALLOWEEN` needs 25 or more days left).

- **The Feed's last temptations**, all in the final week: **Ray Zhao** offers to move everything to the short-video app's shop with a window-squeegee bundle that will go viral and never be bought twice (`THE SQUEEGEE PIVOT`); the Professor offers a "case study slot" in Hexagon 2 (STACK +1); a "40% OFF SITEWIDE" banner is one click away (`sitewide_discount`); and Brayden offers **Halcyon Autopilot**, free for the season: *"You don't need to understand it. That's the point."* (`AUTOPILOT`.)
- **The thread:** drafting a thread about your Black Friday strategy before Black Friday has happened gets a like from Theo in under a minute and a quote-post from the Professor. Turning it into a course is `THE THREAD`.

**ON THE LINE (a post can save it):** **4,000 ceramic sponge holders** in the warehouse that nobody ordered more than 300 of. A post that DOES NUMBERS sells a thousand; VIRAL sells all of them in a day, at full price, and heals one MARGIN level. What goes viral is never the product: it's the sponge with hands, or Dot's handwritten note in a customer's box.

## THANKSGIVING: THE BLACKOUT

**The ad platform has broken** (`rules.md` §14). Spend: $0. Conversions: 0. Every ad: *Learning*. Status page: *"We're aware of an issue affecting some advertisers."* The ads are still running. The money is still spending. Nobody can see anything, and Black Friday starts in ten hours.

Let the panic be real and funny for one turn: Kyle wants to pause everything, Margo wants to know if they're still being charged (yes), Halcyon emails an upgrade offer, Gus's Back Room chat has 140 unread messages, all of which are *"is yours broken too"*, and the player's uncle asks what they do for work again.
