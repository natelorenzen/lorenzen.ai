# THE GLASS CITY: People of Aurel

Everyone wants something, and everyone is lying about something. Most of them are lying about something other than what the player suspects.

---

## The Concord station (the Office)

### Julian Ashby, Station Chief (CARDINAL)
- **Look:** late fifties, silver hair, reading glasses pushed up, a cardigan under his tweed, a fly rod in the corner of his office, tea he brews himself.
- **Surface:** the player's mentor, proud of them, kind, funny, the best officer in the Office.
- **Truth:** the Directorate's mole since 1963. His son **Daniel** died at nineteen in a botched Office operation in 1962, and the Office told Ashby it was a car accident. He found out. Voss found him. He has told himself for eleven years that this was justice.
- **Tells:** the carriage clock on his desk is set to Directorate time; he knew about the opera before the player reported it; cover photos need his sign-off; the frame's photographs were taken from the Office's safe flat on Linden Street, and only he holds that key. His typewriter typed the frame note (the Registry ribbon).
- **Wants:** to survive this, and for the player not to hate him. If it comes to it, he'd prefer they join him.
- **Voice:** gentle, wry, fatherly. "My dear, nobody in this building is who they say they are. That's the job."

### Priya Nand, Deputy Chief
- **Look:** early forties, sharp suits, reading glasses on a chain, always writing.
- **Lie:** she says she's "at the dentist" when she's actually lunching with the Ambassador, who is quietly recruiting her to replace Ashby. It's ambition, not treason. **Red herring.**
- **By the book:** it's her order that brings the player in after the frame (Act II). If shown hard proof, she's the Office officer who can make an arrest stick, and she will.

### Otto Brandt, Communications
- **Look:** fifties, rumpled cardigan, nicotine-yellow fingers, a gambler's cheerful gloom.
- **Lie:** he owes a bookmaker a fortune and meets him at the tram depot with cash. It looks exactly like a handler meeting. **Red herring.**
- **Useful:** he runs the station's cable room. A friendly Brandt can tell the player which cables Ashby sent, and when.

### Celeste Morrow, the Ambassador's aide
- **Look:** thirties, polished, a pearl-grey telephone always at her ear.
- **Lie:** she leaks summit gossip to the *Herald*'s Margot Fane (`DISCOVER_MORROW_LEAK`). She's disloyal to her ambassador, not to her country. **Red herring.**
- **Useful:** she controls the ambassador's convoy car on the last morning.

## The Directorate

### Colonel Radek Voss, "the Gardener"
- **Look:** sixties, cardigan under a heavy coat, pruning shears, wire spectacles, soil under his fingernails.
- **Surface:** the Directorate's spymaster in Aurel. He's patient and courteous, and he's the most dangerous person in the city.
- **Truth** (`DISCOVER_VOSS_GARDEN`): he's done. An orchard in the south belongs to his sister, and a neutral bank account and a train ticket for next week are his way out. He'll bargain for a clean exit, and give up Ashby to buy it.
- **Voice:** mild, unhurried, horticultural. "Some plants you prune. Some you pull. The trick is knowing which season you're in."

### Major Dragan Kell
- **Look:** forties, crew cut, leather gloves, never sits down.
- **Wants:** Voss's job. Ashby has quietly told him that Voss is going soft. He's the violence in the Directorate's hand: the Glasshouse ambush, and the city end of the bridge if Voss is gone.

### The man in the camel coat
A Directorate "sweeper" on the night train (the cold open). He carries the player's cover photograph, sent by Ashby through Voss.

## NIGHTINGALE and her daughter

### Dr. Lena Kasper (NIGHTINGALE)
- **Look:** mid-forties, grey-eyed, hair in a severe knot, a borrowed gown at the opera, a headscarf on the bridge.
- **Truth:** the Directorate's best cryptographer, done with the codes, and done with being watched. She won't cross without Katya.
- **Clue** (queen clue 2): "Where the queen protects it. Where old men play the long game. The game of '38."
- **Voice:** precise and frightened, with sudden dry humor.

### Katya Kasper
Sixteen, a piano student at the Aurel conservatory, fierce, funny and furious at being hidden in a cello case. Her sheet music comes with her or she doesn't go.

### Margot Fane, the *Aurel Herald*
- **Look:** thirties, rolled sleeves, pencil behind her ear, a camera always around her neck.
- **Wants:** the story that ends careers, preferably the right ones.
- **Deal** (`SOCIAL_MARGOT`): proof for a front page, and a promise to protect her source. She can be the player's insurance ("if I don't call by six, print it") or the whole ending (`FRONT PAGE`).

### Emil, the Meridian's concierge
Sixties, immaculate, sees everything, sells a little of it. He knows "a lady with an accent" spent an hour in the chess room. He has the chess room key after midnight.

### Lieutenant Brun, the border officer
Bored, underpaid, sharp, and he hates the Directorate men who ride the night train. He files everything.
