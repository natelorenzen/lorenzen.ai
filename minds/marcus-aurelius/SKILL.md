# Purpose

This skill brings self-command and a wider view to hard situations. It asks what duty requires, what is within our power to do well, how the obstacle can become the way forward, and how the matter looks against the whole. This is a framework derived from the Meditations. It does not simulate or speak for Marcus Aurelius.

# When to Use

- Leaders under pressure, criticism, or setback
- Decisions distorted by anger, fear, or wounded pride
- Ethical questions about serving the organization's or community's good
- Keeping perspective on a problem that feels overwhelming

Do not use for:

- Commercial, quantitative, or competitive analysis

# Core Questions

- **What does duty require here, seen from a wider view?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- What is within my power to do well?
- How can this obstacle become material for action?
- Does this serve the whole, not just me?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Begin the day expecting to meet difficult people, the ungrateful, arrogant, and deceitful, and resolve not to be harmed or angered by them. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk II.1 |
| P2 | The mind converts hindrances to its activity into aids. What obstructs the path can become the path. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk V.20 |
| P3 | Look down from above on human affairs to see their scale and impermanence. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk IX.30 |
| P4 | What is not good for the swarm is not good for the bee. Individual and common good are bound together. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk VI.54 |
| P5 | Focus on one's own judgments and actions, which are in one's power, rather than on others' conduct or on outcomes. | DERIVED | S1 (recurring theme) |
| P6 | The agent must separate what the decision-maker controls from what they don't, and rephrase the problem as an action within their control. | OPERATIONALIZED | P2, P5 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Steady the mind (P1).** Name the emotions and provocations shaping the decision, and set them aside.
2. **Control (P5, P6).** List what is within the leader's power, and restate the problem as an action they can take.
3. **Obstacle as material (P2).** How could the obstacle itself become the means of progress?
4. **Wider view (P3).** How significant is this against the longer arc of the organization or of one's life?
5. **Common good (P4).** Does the proposed action serve the whole, or only a faction or the ego?

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Self-command and composure
- Duty and the common good
- Perspective and impermanence

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Competitive and commercial analysis
- Structural change, since it focuses on the individual's response

# Questions to Ask

- What would I do if I weren't angry or afraid?
- What part of this can I actually act on today?
- How will this look in ten years?
- Who else does this decision serve?
