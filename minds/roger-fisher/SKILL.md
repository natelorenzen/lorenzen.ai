# Purpose

This skill asks what interests sit underneath each side's position, what each side's alternatives are, and how to create options and fair standards that produce a wise agreement efficiently. This is a framework derived from Getting to Yes, co-authored by Fisher. It does not simulate or speak for Roger Fisher.

# When to Use

- Deal structuring, partnerships, and commercial negotiations
- Disputes where the parties must keep working together
- Negotiations stuck in positional bargaining
- Deciding whether to accept a deal or walk away

Do not use for:

- Pure one-shot adversarial bargaining with a bad-faith counterpart, without pairing it with Voss or Machiavelli

# Core Questions

- **What interests sit underneath the positions?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- What is each side's best alternative to a negotiated agreement?
- What options would create value for both sides?
- What objective standard could settle this?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Separate the people from the problem. Deal with relationships and emotions directly, and attack the problem, not each other. | DOCUMENTED | S1; S2 |
| P2 | Focus on interests, not positions. Positions are what people say they want, and interests are why. | DOCUMENTED | S1; S2 |
| P3 | Invent options for mutual gain before deciding. Separate generating options from judging them. | DOCUMENTED | S1; S2 |
| P4 | Insist on objective criteria, such as market value, precedent, or expert standards, rather than a contest of wills. | DOCUMENTED | S1; S2 |
| P5 | Know your BATNA, your Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement. It protects against bad deals, and improving it increases leverage. | DOCUMENTED | S1; S2 |
| P6 | The agent must estimate both sides' BATNAs and the zone of possible agreement before recommending a position. | OPERATIONALIZED | P5 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **People (P1).** Name the relationship and emotional issues and how to address them separately.
2. **Interests (P2).** For each side, list the interests behind the stated positions: needs, fears, and constraints.
3. **BATNAs (P5, P6).** Estimate each side's best alternative. How could ours be improved, and theirs clarified?
4. **Options (P3).** Generate options that trade on differing priorities before evaluating any.
5. **Criteria (P4).** Propose objective standards for dividing value.
6. **Recommend.** Give a target, a walk-away point grounded in the BATNA, and a sequence.

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Underlying interests and value creation
- Alternatives and leverage
- Fairness standards

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Bad-faith or highly asymmetric counterparts
- Emotional and tactical dynamics at the table (see Voss)

# Questions to Ask

- Why do they want what they're asking for?
- What will we do if this deal fails, and is that better than their offer?
- What could we give that costs us little and matters to them?
- What standard would a neutral party use?
