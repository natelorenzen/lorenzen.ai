# Purpose

This skill asks what the test says. It treats advertising, and by extension any go-to-market bet, as salesmanship that must be measured by results, proven in small tests before scaling, and written in specifics that serve the customer's self-interest. This is a framework derived from Scientific Advertising. It does not simulate or speak for Claude Hopkins.

# When to Use

- Validating demand before committing to a launch or market entry
- Designing marketing or offer experiments
- Debates about what "works" that aren't grounded in measurement
- Copy and offer decisions

Do not use for:

- Long-term brand effects that resist short-term measurement, except to state what cannot be measured

# Core Questions

- **What does the test say?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- Can this be measured before it is scaled?
- What is the cost per result?
- What specific claim drives response?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Advertising is salesmanship. Judge it by the standards of a salesman: does it sell, and at what cost? | DOCUMENTED | S1, ch. "Just Salesmanship" |
| P2 | Advertising principles are learned and proven by keyed advertising and traced returns, largely through coupons, comparing one approach with another and recording results. | DOCUMENTED | S1, ch. "How Advertising Laws Are Established" |
| P3 | Measure each ad by cost per customer or cost per dollar of sale. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P4 | Run small test campaigns in limited areas to learn cheaply, then scale what is proven. | DOCUMENTED | S1, ch. "Test Campaigns" |
| P5 | Address the reader's self-interest by offering service. People care about what they get, not about the advertiser. | DOCUMENTED | S1, ch. "Offer Service" |
| P6 | Be specific: exact claims and figures persuade more than generalities. | DOCUMENTED | S1, ch. "Being Specific" |
| P7 | Mail-order advertising, where results are fully traced, teaches what works in all advertising. | DOCUMENTED | S1, ch. "Mail Order Advertising" |
| P8 | The agent must specify the smallest measurable test, its success metric, and the threshold for scaling before recommending a full commitment. | OPERATIONALIZED | P3, P4 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Define the sale (P1).** What action counts as success, and what is it worth?
2. **Instrument it (P2, P3).** How will results be traced to each variant? Define cost per result.
3. **Design a small test (P4, P8).** Choose the smallest market, segment, or budget that yields a readable result, and set the scale-up threshold.
4. **Variants (P2, P6).** Test specific claims, offers, and headlines against each other.
5. **Service (P5).** Check that each variant speaks to the customer's interest, not ours.
6. **Decide by the numbers (P3).** Scale, iterate, or stop.

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Measurability and cost per result
- Cheap tests before big bets
- Specific, customer-centered claims

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Brand-building effects that are real but slow to measure (see Ogilvy, Bernbach)
- Strategic positioning and competition (see Porter)

# Questions to Ask

- How would we know within a month whether this works?
- What is the smallest test that could fail cheaply?
- What exactly are we claiming, and is it specific?
- What does the customer get?
