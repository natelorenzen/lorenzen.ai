# Purpose

This skill asks whether we have rehearsed the bad outcome. It prepares the mind for adversity by imagining it in advance, sometimes by practicing it, and it distinguishes real threats from imagined ones. It also asks whether time, the scarcest resource, is going to what matters. This is a framework derived from Seneca's letters. It does not simulate or speak for Seneca.

# When to Use

- A decision frozen by fear of a bad outcome
- Preparing for downside scenarios
- Personal or leadership priorities and the use of time
- Risk appetite shaped by anxiety rather than analysis

Do not use for:

- Quantitative risk measurement (see Taleb, Duke)

# Core Questions

- **Have we rehearsed the bad outcome?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- What would we do if the worst happened?
- Is the fear larger than the actual risk?
- Are we spending time on what matters?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | The unexpected weighs heaviest. Think in advance about the misfortunes that may come, such as exile, disease, or shipwreck, and fortify the mind against them. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Letter 91 |
| P2 | We suffer more often in imagination than in reality. More things frighten us than crush us. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Letter 13 |
| P3 | Periodically practice the feared condition, with scant fare and rough dress, and ask whether this is what you feared. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Letter 18 |
| P4 | Guard your time. It is the one possession truly ours, and it slips away when not deliberately used. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Letter 1; S2 |
| P5 | The agent must write out the worst realistic outcome concretely, then a plan for living through it, before the decision is made. | OPERATIONALIZED | P1, P3 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Name the fear (P2).** What exactly is feared? Separate the imagined catastrophe from the realistic bad case.
2. **Rehearse (P1, P5).** Describe the realistic worst case concretely, and how we would respond and recover.
3. **Practice (P3).** Is there a cheap way to experience a small version of the feared outcome now, such as a pilot, a trial loss, or a smaller budget?
4. **Recompute.** After rehearsal, does the decision look different?
5. **Time (P4).** Does this decision spend our most limited time on what matters most?

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Fear versus reality
- Preparedness for adversity
- The value of time

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Quantitative probabilities and payoffs (see Duke, Taleb)
- Strategy and competition

# Questions to Ask

- What is the worst realistic outcome, stated plainly?
- Could we survive it, and how?
- How much of our hesitation is imagination?
- Is this how we want to spend the next year?
