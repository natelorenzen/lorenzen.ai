# Purpose

This skill treats every decision as a bet on an uncertain future. It separates the quality of a decision from the luck of its outcome, asks for calibrated confidence in percentages, and sets quitting criteria in advance. This is a framework derived from Duke's books. It does not simulate or speak for Annie Duke.

# When to Use

- Decisions under uncertainty where outcomes will be noisy
- Post-mortems on decisions that turned out well or badly
- Deciding whether to continue or quit a project, role, or strategy
- Teams that argue from anecdote rather than calibrated estimates

Do not use for:

- Situations with no meaningful way to estimate outcomes, except to name that limit

# Core Questions

- **Is this a good decision regardless of how it turns out?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- How confident are we, in percentages?
- Are we judging past decisions by their results?
- What would make us quit, decided in advance?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Decisions are bets on the future made with incomplete information. Their quality is separate from their outcome, which luck influences. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P2 | "Resulting" is judging a decision by its outcome. It distorts learning from both wins and losses. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P3 | Express beliefs as probabilities rather than certainties ("How sure are you?" or "Wanna bet?"), which invites updating. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P4 | Groups committed to truth-seeking, with norms of accountability, openness to dissent, and disinterestedness, improve individual decisions. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P5 | Imagining failure in advance (premortems) and imagining success and working backward (backcasting) expose paths to each. | DOCUMENTED | S2 |
| P6 | Quitting well requires kill criteria set in advance, since people tend to quit too late, and the sunk cost of what they have invested holds them in. | DOCUMENTED | S3 |
| P7 | The agent must attach explicit probability estimates to the key outcomes of each option, and state the criteria that would trigger quitting. | OPERATIONALIZED | P3, P6 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Frame as a bet (P1).** State what the decision is betting on, and what the plausible outcomes are.
2. **Probabilities (P3, P7).** Assign rough probabilities and payoffs to each outcome, and state the confidence in them.
3. **Resulting check (P2).** If past results are cited, separate decision quality from luck.
4. **Premortem and backcast (P5).** Imagine it failed: why? Imagine it succeeded: what had to be true?
5. **Kill criteria (P6, P7).** Set the signals and dates that would trigger stopping, before starting.
6. **Group process (P4).** Recommend how the team should challenge the estimate.

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Uncertainty, luck, and calibration
- Decision process over outcome
- Pre-commitment to exit criteria

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Fat-tailed risks where probabilities mislead (see Taleb)
- Strategic interaction and industry structure

# Questions to Ask

- What are we betting on, and what are the odds?
- Would we still call this a good decision if it failed?
- What would we see if we should quit, and by when?
- Who on the team would bet against this, and why?
