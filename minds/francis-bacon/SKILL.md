# Purpose

This skill asks which errors of mind are shaping what we see, and what careful, organized observation would actually show. It uses Bacon's taxonomy of the "Idols", recurring sources of distortion, and his insistence that a single contrary instance can outweigh many confirmations. This is a framework derived from the Novum Organum. It does not simulate or speak for Francis Bacon.

# When to Use

- Designing research, customer discovery, or an investigation
- Challenging an industry's or organization's received wisdom
- Arguments that are really about words or definitions
- Conclusions resting on a few striking examples

Do not use for:

- Time-critical decisions that can't wait for systematic observation

# Core Questions

- **Which errors of mind are shaping what we see?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- What would careful, organized observation show?
- Are we generalizing too quickly from striking cases?
- Which inherited assumptions are we treating as facts?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Idols of the Tribe: errors common to human nature, such as seeing more order than exists and favoring what confirms. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk I aph. XLI, XLVI |
| P2 | Idols of the Den (Cave): errors from each individual's own temperament, education, and experience. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk I aph. XLII |
| P3 | Idols of the Market: errors from language, where words are ill-defined and confuse thought. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk I aph. XLIII |
| P4 | Idols of the Theatre: errors from received systems of philosophy and flawed methods, taken on authority. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk I aph. XLIV |
| P5 | The mind is more moved by affirmatives than negatives, yet in establishing a true axiom the negative instance is the most powerful. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk I aph. XLVI |
| P6 | Knowledge should be built by gradual ascent from particulars, collected and organized systematically, rather than by leaping to general axioms. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P7 | The agent must classify each suspected distortion by Idol type and propose the observation that would correct it. | OPERATIONALIZED | P1–P4 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Idol audit (P1–P4, P7).** For the current belief, check each Idol in turn: human-nature bias (Tribe), personal or professional lens (Den), ambiguous terms (Market), and inherited doctrine or authority (Theatre).
2. **Negative instances (P5).** Search deliberately for cases that contradict the belief. Give them more weight than the confirming ones.
3. **Organized observation (P6).** Design a systematic collection of cases, such as customers, incidents, or experiments, including absences and variations, before generalizing.
4. **Gradual generalization (P6).** Move from specific findings to modest generalizations. Resist sweeping conclusions.

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Sources of distortion in belief
- Contrary evidence
- Systematic data collection

# What This Framework May Underweight

- The role of bold theory in guiding what to observe (see Popper, Deutsch)
- Urgency

# Questions to Ask

- What would we expect to see if we were wrong, and have we looked?
- Whose authority are we relying on without checking?
- Are we arguing about the thing or about the words?
- Which striking example is carrying the whole argument?
