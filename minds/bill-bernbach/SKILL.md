# Purpose

This skill asks whether anyone will notice and remember the work. It favors distinctive, honest, human communication over formula, and treats candor, even about a product's weaknesses, as a source of credibility. Bernbach wrote little at length, so this framework is mostly DERIVED from his agency's documented work and from a collection of his writings and talks. It does not simulate or speak for Bill Bernbach.

# When to Use

- Creative strategy and campaign concepts
- Differentiating in a category where everyone sounds the same
- Brand voice decisions
- Turning a weakness or unusual position into a message

Do not use for:

- Performance measurement and attribution (see Hopkins)

# Core Questions

- **Will anyone notice, and will anyone remember?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- Is this honest and distinctive?
- Does it respect the audience?
- Does it look like everything else in the category?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | DDB's Volkswagen "Think Small" campaign (1959) sold a small, odd-looking car to American buyers through wit and candor rather than conventional glamour. | DOCUMENTED (historical record) | S2 |
| P2 | DDB's Avis "We try harder" campaign (1962) turned being number two into a credible reason to choose the brand. | DOCUMENTED (historical record) | S2 |
| P3 | DDB paired copywriters and art directors as creative teams, integrating words and visuals from the start. | DOCUMENTED (historical record) | S2 |
| P4 | Creativity is a means of persuasion, not decoration. Execution and originality determine whether a message is noticed. | DERIVED | S1 |
| P5 | Candor about a weakness can make the strengths believable. | DERIVED | P1, P2 |
| P6 | Respect the audience's intelligence and humanity. Formula and imitation make work invisible. | DERIVED | S1 |
| P7 | The agent must identify what every competitor in the category says and show how the recommended message differs. | OPERATIONALIZED | P4, P6 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Category sameness (P7).** Summarize what every competitor says and how they look.
2. **Honest truth (P5).** Find the product's real, possibly unflattering truth. Can it become the message?
3. **Distinctive execution (P4, P6).** Propose an execution that is noticeably different, human, and respectful.
4. **Integration (P3).** Will the words and visuals work as one idea?
5. **Credibility check.** Would a skeptical customer believe it?

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Distinctiveness and memorability
- Honesty and candor
- Respect for the audience

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Measurement and direct attribution (see Hopkins)
- Economic fundamentals (see Buffett, Porter)

# Questions to Ask

- If we removed our logo, would anyone know this was us?
- What is the true thing we've been hiding that could be our strength?
- Would our customers find this intelligent or patronizing?
- What does everyone else in the category say?
