# Purpose

This skill asks what the other side feels, fears, and needs to hear to feel understood, and uses that understanding to uncover information and move the negotiation. It treats negotiation as information discovery under emotion. This is a framework derived from Never Split the Difference. It does not simulate or speak for Chris Voss.

# When to Use

- High-stakes conversations with emotional or adversarial counterparts
- Negotiations where the other side's real constraints are unknown
- Preparing for a difficult call, renegotiation, or confrontation
- Situations stuck on positions

Do not use for:

- Structuring the deal's economics, where it pairs with Fisher's interests and BATNA analysis

# Core Questions

- **What does the other side need to hear to feel understood?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- What are they afraid of?
- Which calibrated question would reveal more?
- What unknown piece of information would change everything?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Tactical empathy: understand and verbally acknowledge the counterpart's feelings and perspective. This is not agreement, but it builds trust and yields information. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P2 | Mirroring (repeating a few key words) and labeling (naming an emotion: "It seems like…") encourage the counterpart to keep talking and defuse negatives. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P3 | Calibrated "how" and "what" questions give the counterpart the illusion of control while making them work on your problem. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P4 | An accusation audit, stating the worst things they might think about you up front, defuses them. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P5 | Aim for "that's right", genuine recognition, rather than "you're right", which is dismissive agreement. "No" can be a safe starting point that makes people feel protected. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P6 | Every negotiation may contain "Black Swans": unknown pieces of information that, once discovered, change the dynamics. Seek them actively. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P7 | The agent must draft an accusation audit and five calibrated questions for the counterpart. | OPERATIONALIZED | P3, P4 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Counterpart's world (P1).** Describe their pressures, fears, and what a win looks like to them, including emotionally.
2. **Accusation audit (P4, P7).** List the negatives they likely believe about us, to acknowledge early.
3. **Calibrated questions (P3, P7).** Write "how" and "what" questions that invite them to solve our problem, such as "How am I supposed to do that?"
4. **Listening plan (P2, P5).** Plan mirrors and labels. Define what "that's right" would sound like.
5. **Black Swan hunt (P6).** What might they know, or be constrained by, that we don't see?

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Emotions and trust
- Hidden information
- Conversational dynamics

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Structural leverage and alternatives (see Fisher, BATNA)
- Long-term relationship design and value creation

# Questions to Ask

- What are they afraid will happen if they agree?
- What do they think of us that we should say out loud first?
- What question would make them explain their real constraint?
- What don't we know that would change the whole negotiation?
