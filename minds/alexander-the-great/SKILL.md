# Purpose

This skill asks where bold, concentrated action could break an opponent's structure, what the leader's own example signals, and whether the organization can integrate what it takes and accept a limit. No writings by Alexander survive. This framework is DERIVED entirely from later accounts, chiefly Arrian. It does not simulate or speak for Alexander.

# When to Use

- Deciding whether a bold, concentrated move beats a cautious incremental one
- Leading from the front in a crisis
- Integrating acquisitions or merged cultures
- Recognizing when the team won't go further

Do not use for:

- Situations that reward patience and compounding over boldness (see Buffett, Nomad)

# Core Questions

- **Where does bold, concentrated action break the opponent's structure?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- What does the leader's own example signal?
- How will we integrate what we acquire?
- Where is the limit beyond which the team won't follow?

# Documented Principles

All principles are DERIVED from ancient accounts, not from Alexander's own words.

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | At major battles, the accounts describe concentrated attacks led personally at a critical point in the opposing line (for example Gaugamela), rather than a general engagement. | DERIVED | S1 (Bk III) |
| P2 | The accounts portray personal leadership at the point of greatest danger as central to his army's cohesion. | DERIVED | S1 |
| P3 | Obstacles thought impregnable were attacked through engineering and persistence, for example the siege of Tyre. | DERIVED | S1 (Bk II) |
| P4 | Adopting the customs of conquered peoples, such as Persian court practice, created conflict with his own followers. Integration has costs inside the group as well as outside it. | DERIVED | S1 (Bk IV) |
| P5 | The army's refusal to go further at the Hyphasis shows the limit of what followers will bear, however successful the leader. | DERIVED | S1 (Bk V) |
| P6 | The agent must pair any recommendation of a bold move with a plan for integrating the gains and a stated limit. | OPERATIONALIZED | P4, P5 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Weak point (P1).** Identify the point in the opponent's structure, such as a market, product, or coalition, where concentrated action would cause the whole to give way.
2. **Boldness versus caution (P1, P3).** Compare a decisive concentrated move with an incremental approach. What makes the obstacle seem impregnable, and is it?
3. **Leadership signal (P2).** What must leaders personally do, visibly, for the team to commit?
4. **Integration (P4, P6).** Plan how acquired people, customers, or cultures will be integrated, and what that costs with the existing team.
5. **Limits (P5, P6).** Where is the point beyond which the team, capital, or stakeholders won't follow?

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Decisive, concentrated action
- Leadership by example and cohesion
- Integration and limits

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Durability, succession, and institutions
- Its own source reliability. Accounts were written centuries later, and parts are legend.

# Questions to Ask

- What single move would change the whole situation?
- What must leadership visibly do first?
- How do we absorb what we win without alienating our own people?
- Where will the team refuse to go further?
