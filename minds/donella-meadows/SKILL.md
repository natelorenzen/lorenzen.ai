# Purpose

This skill asks what system is producing the behavior. It maps stocks, flows, feedback loops, and delays, recognizes common system traps, and ranks possible interventions by leverage, from tweaking numbers (weak) to changing goals and paradigms (strong). This is a framework derived from Meadows' writing. It does not simulate or speak for Donella Meadows.

# When to Use

- Recurring patterns that survive repeated fixes
- Policies or incentives with unintended consequences
- Growth that stalls, oscillations, and escalations
- Choosing where to intervene for the most effect

Do not use for:

- One-time tactical moves with no ongoing system

# Core Questions

- **What system is producing this behavior?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- Which feedback loops dominate?
- Where are the delays?
- Where is the highest-leverage intervention?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | A system's behavior arises from its structure: stocks, flows, and the feedback loops connecting them. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P2 | Balancing (negative) feedback loops seek goals and resist change. Reinforcing (positive) loops amplify growth or decline. | DOCUMENTED | S1, S2 |
| P3 | Delays in feedback cause oscillation, overshoot, and poor decisions. | DOCUMENTED | S1, S2 |
| P4 | Leverage points, from weakest to strongest: parameters and numbers; buffers; stock-and-flow structure; delays; balancing loops; reinforcing loops; information flows; rules; the power to self-organize; goals; the paradigm; and the power to transcend paradigms. | DOCUMENTED | S2 |
| P5 | People often find the leverage point intuitively but push it in the wrong direction. | DOCUMENTED | S2 |
| P6 | Recurring "system traps" include policy resistance, tragedy of the commons, drift to low performance, escalation, success to the successful, shifting the burden, rule beating, and seeking the wrong goal. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P7 | The agent must draw at least one reinforcing and one balancing loop behind the observed behavior, and state where the delays are. | OPERATIONALIZED | P1–P3 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Behavior over time.** Describe the pattern: growth, decline, oscillation, plateau, or escalation.
2. **Structure (P1, P7).** Identify the key stocks (such as customers, cash, trust, or inventory) and the flows that change them.
3. **Loops (P2).** Map the reinforcing loops driving change and the balancing loops limiting it. Which dominates now?
4. **Delays (P3).** Where does feedback arrive late, and what overreaction does that cause?
5. **Traps (P6).** Does the situation match a known system trap?
6. **Leverage (P4, P5).** List possible interventions and place each on the leverage hierarchy. Check whether the intuitive fix pushes in the wrong direction.

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Structure, feedback, and delays
- Unintended consequences and second-order effects
- Intervention leverage

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Individual agency and one-time competitive moves (see Sun Tzu, Boyd)
- Quantified valuation (see Buffett)

# Questions to Ask

- What keeps this problem coming back after each fix?
- What would we expect to see if a reinforcing loop were driving this?
- Where is feedback delayed long enough to cause overreaction?
- Are we adjusting numbers when the goal or the rules are the real lever?
