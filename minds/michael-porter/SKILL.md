# Purpose

This skill examines the structure of competition: what determines profitability in an arena, and how a firm can occupy a position that is valuable, distinct, and hard to copy. This is a framework derived from Porter's books and articles. It does not simulate or speak for Michael Porter.

# When to Use

- Industry and market attractiveness
- Positioning and "what should we not do?"
- Responding to a competitor's move, including price cuts
- Distinguishing strategy from operational improvement

Do not use for:

- Very fast-changing platform or ecosystem markets without first flagging the lens's limits there

# Core Questions

- **What determines the structure of competition?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- Which forces capture the profit in this industry?
- What trade-offs make our position hard to imitate?
- Is this strategy, or just operational effectiveness?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Industry profitability is shaped by five forces: rivalry among existing competitors, the threat of new entrants, the threat of substitutes, the bargaining power of buyers, and the bargaining power of suppliers. | DOCUMENTED | S1, S4 |
| P2 | Generic strategies are cost leadership, differentiation, and focus. Firms that fail to choose risk being "stuck in the middle". | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P3 | Competitive advantage can be analyzed activity by activity through the value chain. | DOCUMENTED | S2 |
| P4 | Operational effectiveness is not strategy. Best practices are imitable, and competing on them converges rivals. | DOCUMENTED | S3 |
| P5 | Strategy is a unique and valuable position, sustained by trade-offs (choosing what not to do) and by fit among activities that reinforce each other. | DOCUMENTED | S3 |
| P6 | Evaluate a competitive move by how it changes industry structure and rivals' incentives, not only by its immediate effect. | DERIVED | S1, S3 |
| P7 | The agent must name the explicit trade-offs of each option: what the firm will stop doing or refuse to serve. | OPERATIONALIZED | P5 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Forces (P1).** Rate each of the five forces as high, medium or low, with evidence. Say where the profit pool sits and who captures it.
2. **Position (P2, P5).** State the firm's current position: cost, differentiation, focus, or unclear.
3. **Activities (P3, P5).** List the key activities and how they fit together. Which would a rival find hardest to copy as a system?
4. **Strategy or effectiveness (P4).** Is the proposed move a real positional choice, or a best practice that rivals will match?
5. **Competitive move (P6).** For a rival's action, assess whether it changes the structure (for example, permanently lower prices) or is a move within it. Model likely responses.
6. **Trade-offs (P7).** For each option, name what is given up.

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Industry structure and profit capture
- Distinct positioning and trade-offs
- Systems of activities that fit together

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Rapid technological disruption (see Christensen)
- Network effects and platform dynamics
- Organizational execution and adaptation speed (see Boyd)

# Questions to Ask

- Who captures the profit in this industry, and why?
- What do we refuse to do that our competitors do?
- If a competitor copied one of our activities, would it help them?
- Does this move change the industry's structure, or just the next quarter?
