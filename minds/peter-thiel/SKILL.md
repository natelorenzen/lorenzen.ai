# Purpose

This skill tests whether a venture or strategy escapes competition or is trapped inside it. It asks what the business knows that others don't, whether it can dominate a small market first, and whether the plan is definite or a hopeful spread of bets. This is a framework derived from Zero to One. It does not simulate or speak for Peter Thiel.

# When to Use

- New ventures, new products, and market-entry choices
- Strategies that look like "compete harder in a crowded market"
- Investment theses where outcomes follow a power law
- Checking whether a plan is specific or merely optimistic

Do not use for:

- Mature commodity businesses where differentiation genuinely isn't available, except to say so

# Core Questions

- **Are we escaping competition or trapped inside it?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- What important truth do we believe that few others agree with?
- Which small market can we dominate first, and how do we expand from it?
- Is our plan definite, or an indefinite hope?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Perfect competition drives profits to zero. Durable value comes from monopoly-like positions built on something different, not better execution of the same thing. | DOCUMENTED | S1 (chs. 3–4) |
| P2 | Monopoly characteristics: proprietary technology (roughly an order of magnitude better), network effects, economies of scale, and branding. | DOCUMENTED | S1 (ch. 5) |
| P3 | Start by dominating a small market, then expand in concentric circles. Avoid framing the goal as "disrupting" incumbents. | DOCUMENTED | S1 (ch. 5) |
| P4 | Value comes from secrets: important truths that few people agree with. The contrarian question asks what important truth you believe that few agree with. | DOCUMENTED | S1 (chs. 1, 8) |
| P5 | Definite optimism, meaning a concrete plan for a better future, beats indefinite optimism, which spreads bets because no one knows what to do. | DOCUMENTED | S1 (ch. 6) |
| P6 | Venture outcomes follow a power law. A few results dominate everything, so each bet should be capable of returning the whole portfolio. | DOCUMENTED | S1 (ch. 7) |
| P7 | Rivalry makes firms fixate on each other and lose sight of what is valuable. | DOCUMENTED | S1 (ch. 4) |
| P8 | Seven questions to test a business: engineering, timing, monopoly, people, distribution, durability, and secret. | DOCUMENTED | S1 (ch. 13) |
| P9 | The agent must state the specific market the plan can dominate, and its size, before assessing expansion. | OPERATIONALIZED | P3 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Competition check (P1, P7).** Is this a crowded market where the plan is to out-execute? Or is the plan to be different? Name the rivals the team is fixated on.
2. **Secret (P4).** State the non-consensus insight the strategy depends on. If there is none, say so.
3. **Monopoly sources (P2).** Assess proprietary technology, network effects, scale, and brand. Which exist, and which could be built?
4. **Small market first (P3, P9).** Identify the smallest market the plan can dominate, and the sequence of expansion.
5. **Definite plan (P5).** Is there a specific plan, or an optimistic spread of options?
6. **Power law (P6).** Could this outcome be very large, or only incrementally good?
7. **Seven questions (P8).** Run the checklist and flag the weakest answer.

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Differentiation and escape from competition
- Contrarian, specific insight
- Scale of the potential outcome

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Incremental improvement in competitive markets, where it can be the right strategy
- Regulatory and antitrust exposure of monopoly strategies
- Downside protection (see Buffett, Taleb)

# Questions to Ask

- What do we know that the market doesn't?
- Who else could do exactly this, and why won't they?
- Which market is small enough to own completely?
- If this works, is it huge, or just fine?
