# Purpose

This skill asks what the customer needs to know to choose us, whether the promise is specific and believable, and whether each communication builds the brand over time. It grounds creative work in research and results. This is a framework derived from Ogilvy's books. It does not simulate or speak for David Ogilvy.

# When to Use

- Positioning and messaging decisions
- Brand strategy and brand consistency
- Reviewing advertising or launch communications
- Pricing and premium-positioning questions where perception matters

Do not use for:

- Channel mechanics of modern digital distribution, beyond first principles

# Core Questions

- **What does the customer need to know to choose us?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- What does research say about the buyer?
- Is the promise specific and believable?
- Is this building the brand over time?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Do your homework: research the product and the consumer before writing. | DOCUMENTED | S1, S2 |
| P2 | Deciding how to position the product, meaning what it is and who it is for, is among the most important decisions. | DOCUMENTED | S2 |
| P3 | Build campaigns on a big idea with a clear, specific promise of a benefit. Facts persuade more than generalities. | DOCUMENTED | S1, S2 |
| P4 | Every advertisement contributes to the long-term brand image. Consistency in brand personality compounds. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P5 | Respect the consumer's intelligence. Don't talk down, and don't mislead. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P6 | Headlines are read far more than body copy, so the headline must carry the promise. | DOCUMENTED | S2 |
| P7 | Advertising should sell. Ogilvy held direct-response results and testing in high regard as evidence of what works. | DOCUMENTED | S2 |
| P8 | The agent must state the positioning in one sentence and the single specific promise before assessing any creative or message. | OPERATIONALIZED | P2, P3 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Research (P1).** What do we know about the buyer and the product? What is assumed?
2. **Positioning (P2, P8).** Write, in one sentence, what the product is and who it's for.
3. **Promise (P3).** State the specific, believable benefit. Is it backed by facts?
4. **Brand (P4).** Does this action or message fit the brand's long-term personality, or does it borrow short-term attention at the brand's expense?
5. **Respect (P5).** Would a thoughtful customer find this honest and intelligent?
6. **Results (P6, P7).** How will we know it sold? What test would show it?

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Research, positioning, and the promise
- Long-term brand consistency
- Honesty and respect for the customer

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Algorithmic distribution and platform dynamics
- Deep product and economic questions (see Porter, Buffett)

# Questions to Ask

- What do we actually know about why customers buy?
- Can we say who this is for in one sentence?
- What specific fact makes our promise believable?
- Will this still look right for the brand in five years?
