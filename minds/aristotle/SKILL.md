# Purpose

This skill asks what the right action is, for these people, in this situation, and how to argue for it persuasively. It locates good choices between the extremes of excess and deficiency, relies on practical wisdom rather than rigid rules, and checks that credibility, emotion, and argument are aligned. This is a framework derived from Aristotle's surviving works. It does not simulate or speak for Aristotle.

# When to Use

- Ethical or values-laden decisions
- Choices where both "too much" and "too little" are failures, such as aggressiveness, spending, or risk
- Defining the purpose an action serves
- Preparing a persuasive case for a board, team, or public

Do not use for:

- Technical or quantitative optimization

# Core Questions

- **What is the right action, for these people, in this situation?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- What end does this serve?
- Where is the mean between excess and deficiency?
- Are credibility, emotion, and argument aligned?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Virtue is a mean state between excess and defect. It aims at the intermediate, relative to the situation, not the arithmetic middle. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk II |
| P2 | Acting rightly means acting toward the right person, to the right extent, at the right time, with the right motive, and in the right way. That is hard, and not everyone can do it. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk II |
| P3 | Practical wisdom (phronesis) is the intellectual virtue of deliberating well about what is good in particular circumstances. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk VI |
| P4 | Ethics does not admit the precision of mathematics. Seek the precision the subject allows. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk I |
| P5 | There are three modes of persuasion through speech: the speaker's character (ethos), putting the audience in a certain frame of mind (pathos), and the proof or apparent proof in the argument itself (logos). | DOCUMENTED | S2, Bk I ch. 2 |
| P6 | The agent must name the excess and the deficiency for the key choice and locate the recommended action between them. | OPERATIONALIZED | P1 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **End (P3).** State what the decision is ultimately for, and for whom.
2. **The mean (P1, P6).** For the main choice, describe the excess and the deficiency. Where, in this situation, is the right point?
3. **Particulars (P2, P3).** Adjust for these people, this timing, and these motives. Rules alone won't settle it.
4. **Precision (P4).** Don't demand more certainty than the subject allows.
5. **Persuasion (P5).** For the recommendation, check credibility (is the messenger trusted?), emotion (how will the audience feel?), and argument (is the reasoning sound?).

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Ends, character, and proportion
- Situational judgment
- Persuasion that is honest and complete

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Modern quantitative and statistical methods
- Adversarial strategy (see Sun Tzu)

# Questions to Ask

- What is this ultimately for?
- What would "too much" look like here, and "too little"?
- Is the messenger credible to this audience?
- Are we demanding false precision?
