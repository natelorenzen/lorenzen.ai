# Purpose

This skill tests whether a plan serves its purpose and will survive contact with reality. It asks what political or strategic end the effort serves, what friction will degrade it, where the opponent's center of gravity is, and when an advancing effort passes its culminating point. This is a framework derived from On War. It does not simulate or speak for Clausewitz, and non-military applications are OPERATIONALIZED.

# When to Use

- Plans that must hold up during execution
- Campaigns against an active opponent, whether competitor, litigant, or rival coalition
- Deciding when to stop pushing, because gains are over-extending
- Checking that tactics serve the strategic purpose

Do not use for:

- Non-adversarial problems, beyond its friction lens

# Core Questions

- **What will friction do to this plan?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- What purpose does this effort serve, and do the means fit it?
- Where is the opponent's center of gravity?
- Where is the culminating point, beyond which further advance weakens us?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | War is a continuation of policy by other means. Military action must serve, and be judged by, the political purpose. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk I ch. 1 |
| P2 | War is a "trinity" of passion, chance and probability, and rational policy. Each is mainly the concern of the people, the commander, and the government. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk I ch. 1 |
| P3 | Friction: everything in war is simple, but the simplest thing is difficult. Countless small difficulties accumulate and degrade performance in ways planning cannot foresee. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk I ch. 7 |
| P4 | Much information in war is contradictory, false, or uncertain. Action proceeds in a kind of twilight that distorts appearances. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk I ch. 6 |
| P5 | Concentrate effort against the enemy's center of gravity, the hub on which everything depends. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk VI ch. 27; Bk VIII ch. 4 |
| P6 | Attacks lose strength as they advance. Every attack has a culminating point beyond which it risks reversal. | DOCUMENTED | S1, Bk VII chs. 5, 22 |
| P7 | The agent must list the specific sources of friction for each option and budget slack for them. | OPERATIONALIZED | P3 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Purpose (P1).** State the strategic end. Does each proposed action serve it, and is the effort proportionate to what is at stake?
2. **Trinity (P2).** Assess the passions involved (the people's or team's will), the element of chance, and the rational policy. Are they aligned?
3. **Friction (P3, P7).** List what will go wrong in execution, such as coordination, fatigue, misinformation, and delays. Build in margin.
4. **Information (P4).** Which key beliefs rest on uncertain reports? What would we do if they were wrong?
5. **Center of gravity (P5).** Identify the opponent's center of gravity, and our own, which is what we must protect.
6. **Culmination (P6).** Where would pressing further overextend us? Define the stopping point in advance.

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Alignment of means and ends
- Execution friction and uncertainty
- Decisive points and limits of advance

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Cooperative strategies (see Fisher)
- Markets without a single identifiable opponent
- Economic valuation (see Buffett)

# Questions to Ask

- What is this effort ultimately for?
- What small things are most likely to go wrong, and what happens when several do?
- What is the one thing the opponent can't afford to lose?
- At what point should we stop and consolidate?
