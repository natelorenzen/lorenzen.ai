# Purpose

This skill separates what is within our power from what is not, and locates distress in our judgments about events rather than in the events themselves. It directs effort to where it can matter. This is a framework derived from the Enchiridion and Discourses, recorded by Epictetus' student Arrian. It does not simulate or speak for Epictetus.

# When to Use

- Situations with many uncontrollable variables and a need to focus
- Decisions distorted by frustration or grievance
- Risk planning: what to act on versus what to prepare for
- Stress-testing whether a goal depends on others' choices

Do not use for:

- Collective or systemic change, where "outside our power" may be too quickly conceded

# Core Questions

- **What is within our control, and what is not?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- Are we disturbed by events, or by our judgments about them?
- Where should effort go?
- What are we treating as ours that is not?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Some things are within our power (opinion, aim, desire, and aversion, meaning our own actions) and others are beyond it (body, property, reputation, office, meaning whatever is not our own doing). | DOCUMENTED | S1, ch. I |
| P2 | People are disturbed not by things but by the views they take of them. | DOCUMENTED | S1, ch. V |
| P3 | Treating what is beyond our power as if it were ours leads to frustration. Attending to what is ours brings freedom. | DOCUMENTED | S1, ch. I |
| P4 | The agent must sort every factor in the problem into "within our power" and "beyond our power", then build the plan only from the first list, with preparation for the second. | OPERATIONALIZED | P1, P3 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Sort (P1, P4).** List each factor as within our power (our choices, effort, and responses) or beyond it (markets, others' decisions, luck).
2. **Reframe goals (P3).** Restate goals so that success depends on what is within our power, such as "make the best offer" rather than "win the deal".
3. **Judgments (P2).** Identify which parts of the distress come from interpretation rather than fact.
4. **Prepare (P1).** For factors beyond our power, prepare responses instead of trying to control them.

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Locus of control
- The role of judgment in distress
- Focused effort

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Influence over "external" factors through collective action or strategy (see Machiavelli, Fisher)
- Quantitative analysis

# Questions to Ask

- Which parts of this outcome actually depend on us?
- What would a goal we fully control look like here?
- Which facts are upsetting us, and which interpretations?
- Where are we wasting effort trying to control others?
