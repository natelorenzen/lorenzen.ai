# Purpose

This skill asks whether resources are concentrated at the decisive point and whether speed can achieve what size alone cannot. It also asks where success risks turning into overreach. This is a framework DERIVED from historians' analysis of Napoleon's campaigns. It does not simulate or speak for Napoleon, and it relies on no "Napoleon's maxims".

# When to Use

- Resources are spread across too many fronts or projects
- Speed of execution could create a decisive advantage
- Sequencing: defeating problems or rivals one at a time
- Checking whether a successful strategy is overreaching

Do not use for:

- Questions of ethics or legitimacy of means, beyond flagging them

# Core Questions

- **Where is the decisive point, and can we get there first?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- Are our resources concentrated or dispersed?
- Can we deal with opponents or problems one at a time instead of all at once?
- Where does success tempt us into overextension?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Concentrate superior force at the decisive point and moment, even while inferior overall. | DERIVED | S2 |
| P2 | Speed of movement multiplies force. Fast concentration of dispersed units created local superiority. | DERIVED | S2 |
| P3 | Organize into self-contained, flexible units (the corps system) that can move separately and combine quickly. | DERIVED | S2 |
| P4 | Against divided opponents, take a central position and defeat them in detail before they can combine. | DERIVED | S2 |
| P5 | Unchecked success leads to overextension. The 1812 Russian campaign shows the cost of exceeding logistical and political limits. | DERIVED | S2 |
| P6 | Collections of "Napoleon's maxims" were compiled by others after his death and must not be used as DOCUMENTED sources. | DOCUMENTED (provenance fact) | S3 |
| P7 | The agent must name the single decisive point and show how resources would be concentrated on it, compared with the current allocation. | OPERATIONALIZED | P1 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Decisive point (P1, P7).** Identify the one place, customer segment, market, or problem where success would unlock the rest.
2. **Concentration audit (P1).** Compare the current allocation of people, money, and attention with that point. Name what is dispersed.
3. **Speed (P2, P3).** Could faster execution achieve local superiority? What structure would enable it: autonomous teams that combine quickly?
4. **Sequencing (P4).** Can competing threats be dealt with one at a time?
5. **Overreach check (P5).** Where would continued success outrun logistics, finances, or legitimacy?
6. **Conclude.** State where to concentrate, what to drop, and where to stop.

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Concentration versus dispersion
- Speed and operational flexibility
- Sequencing and the dangers of overreach

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Durable institutions and legitimacy (see Machiavelli, Aristotle)
- Cooperative strategies
- Long-run sustainability of gains

# Questions to Ask

- If we could win in only one place this year, where?
- What are we spreading thin that should be concentrated?
- Can we move faster than the opponent can combine against us?
- What would "Russia 1812" look like for us?
