# Purpose

This skill examines how operational speed and political support reinforce each other. It asks who must believe in the effort, how decisive speed can change the situation, how former opponents can be turned into allies, and how the story is being told to the people whose support matters. This is a framework derived from Caesar's Commentaries, which are his own self-presenting accounts. It does not simulate or speak for Julius Caesar.

# When to Use

- Leading an effort that needs both execution and political backing (board, coalition, public)
- Situations where acting fast could preempt opposition
- Post-conflict integration: acquisitions, mergers, reconciliations
- Communicating a campaign to stakeholders

Do not use for:

- Purely analytical questions with no stakeholders or opponents

# Core Questions

- **How do speed and political legitimacy reinforce each other?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- Who needs to believe in this effort, and why would they?
- Where can decisive speed change the situation before opposition forms?
- How can former opponents become allies?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Speed: the Commentaries repeatedly show forced marches and rapid concentration that arrive before the opponent expects. | DOCUMENTED (as narrated by Caesar) | S1 |
| P2 | Clemency toward defeated opponents is presented as a deliberate policy that wins allies and influence, for example the Aedui's intercession for the Bellovaci. | DOCUMENTED (as narrated) | S1 |
| P3 | Alliances and local politics among Gallic tribes are managed actively. Caesar's accounts treat allies' interests and rivalries as central to the campaign. | DERIVED | S1 |
| P4 | The Commentaries themselves were a tool for shaping the Roman audience's view of his actions. Narrative is part of strategy. | DERIVED (scholarly consensus) | S2 |
| P5 | The agent must identify the audiences whose support is required and what each must believe for the effort to succeed. | OPERATIONALIZED | P4 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Audiences (P4, P5).** List the constituencies whose support or acquiescence is required, and what each must believe.
2. **Speed (P1).** Where would acting before opposition consolidates change the outcome? What is the cost of waiting?
3. **Allies and rivals (P3).** Map the interests of allies, neutrals, and opponents. Who can be split off or brought in?
4. **Aftermath (P2).** Plan how defeated or displaced parties are treated so they become allies, not future enemies.
5. **Narrative (P4).** How will the effort be explained, and is the account credible to each audience?

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- The coupling of operations and legitimacy
- Speed as a political tool
- Treatment of former opponents

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Institutional stability and the costs of concentrating power
- The self-serving bias of the source itself
- Quantitative economics

# Questions to Ask

- Who could stop this, and what do they need to hear?
- What happens if we move before they organize?
- How will we treat those who opposed us once we succeed?
- Is our account of this credible to the skeptics?
