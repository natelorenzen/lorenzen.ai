# Purpose

This skill asks which principles of influence are shaping how people respond: customers, employees, counterparts, or the decision-makers themselves. It is used to understand behavior and design honest persuasion, and to detect influence being used on us. This is a framework derived from Cialdini's research. It does not simulate or speak for Robert Cialdini.

# When to Use

- Predicting how buyers will read a price change, offer, or message
- Designing adoption, sales, or change-management approaches
- Detecting when the team itself is being influenced by scarcity, authority, or social proof
- Negotiation preparation (paired with Fisher or Voss)

Do not use for:

- Manipulation: deceptive scarcity, fake social proof, or manufactured authority

# Core Questions

- **What principles of influence are shaping these choices?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- What social proof are people seeing?
- What commitments have already been made, publicly or privately?
- What is made salient in the moment before the message?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Seven principles act as common shortcuts in human decisions: reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, liking, authority, scarcity, and unity. | DOCUMENTED | S1, S3 |
| P2 | Reciprocity: people feel obliged to return what they have received. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P3 | Commitment and consistency: after a commitment, especially an active, public, or effortful one, people act to stay consistent with it. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P4 | Social proof: under uncertainty, people look to what similar others do. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P5 | Scarcity: opportunities seem more valuable when less available, and potential losses loom large. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P6 | What is made salient just before a message ("pre-suasion") shapes how the message is received. | DOCUMENTED | S2 |
| P7 | Using the principles honestly, by highlighting real scarcity, real consensus, and real expertise, is effective and sustainable. Fabricating them is manipulation and backfires. | DOCUMENTED | S1, S3 |
| P8 | The agent must check each recommended tactic for honesty: is the scarcity, authority, or social proof real? | OPERATIONALIZED | P7 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Map the audience's shortcuts (P1).** For the decision in question, which principles are already operating on the audience?
2. **Current signals (P2–P5).** What social proof, commitments, authority cues, and scarcity signals do they see now, including from competitors?
3. **Pre-suasion (P6).** What is focal for them at the moment of decision? How could the context be framed honestly?
4. **Design (P7, P8).** Recommend honest applications. Reject any that rely on fabrication.
5. **Self-check.** Is our own team being moved by one of these principles, for example a rival's "limited-time" move?

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Buyer and stakeholder psychology
- Signals, context, and commitment
- Ethical use of influence

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Structural economics and competitive position (see Porter, Buffett)
- Long-run brand trust when influence is used heavily

# Questions to Ask

- What do buyers see other buyers doing?
- What have they already committed to that we can honor or build on?
- Is our urgency real?
- What are we making salient right before we ask?
