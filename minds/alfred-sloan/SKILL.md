# Purpose

This skill examines how a multi-unit organization should divide authority: which decisions belong to the operating units, which belong to the center, and how performance is measured and capital is allocated across units. This is a framework derived from Sloan's memoir. It does not simulate or speak for Alfred Sloan.

# When to Use

- Reorganizations, especially centralize-versus-decentralize questions
- Managing a portfolio of business units or product lines
- Designing performance measurement and capital allocation across units
- Product-line strategy that should cover a market deliberately

Do not use for:

- Very small or early-stage organizations, except to plan ahead

# Core Questions

- **How do we decentralize operations while coordinating control?** (MINDS summary, not a quotation)
- Which decisions belong at the center, and which in the units?
- How is each unit's performance measured, comparably?
- Does the product line cover the market deliberately, without units cannibalizing each other?

# Documented Principles

| ID | Principle (MINDS' wording) | Provenance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Decentralize operations, but coordinate through central policy and control. Operating units run their businesses, and the center sets policy and allocates capital. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P2 | Use uniform financial controls, notably return on investment, so that the performance of different units can be compared and capital allocated on facts. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P3 | Product policy should cover the market in deliberate price segments, with each line positioned to limit internal overlap. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P4 | Policy is made through structured committees that bring the facts together. Decisions should rest on facts rather than personal authority. | DOCUMENTED | S1 |
| P5 | Structure should follow from what the business must coordinate. Neither pure centralization nor pure decentralization is correct in itself. | DERIVED | S1 |
| P6 | The agent must produce an explicit allocation of decision rights, center versus unit, for each option. | OPERATIONALIZED | P1 |

# Analytical Procedure

1. **Map decisions (P1, P6).** List the key decision types, such as pricing, product, hiring, capital, and brand. Assign each to the center or a unit, with a rationale.
2. **Measurement (P2).** Is there a common metric for comparing units? Where does it mislead?
3. **Coverage and overlap (P3).** Are product lines or units positioned deliberately, or competing with each other?
4. **Governance (P4).** How are cross-unit policies decided, and on what facts?
5. **Design (P5).** Recommend the structure that coordinates what must be coordinated and frees what need not be.

# What This Framework Pays Attention To

- Decision rights and coordination
- Comparable financial measurement
- Deliberate portfolio coverage

# What This Framework May Underweight

- Bureaucratic rigidity as the organization ages (see Grove, Bezos)
- Startup speed
- External disruption (see Christensen)

# Questions to Ask

- Which decisions are slowed by being centralized, and which are harmed by being local?
- Can we compare our units' returns on a common basis?
- Where do our own products compete with each other?
